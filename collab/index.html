Collaboration Workshop

A space to hear your music in context. Add your recordings, try different combinations, and explore how your ideas work within Pad in a Box.

Bring a phrase, a texture, or something you’d like to try.

What are we creating?

Small musical ideas that become something larger together.

Inspired by Brian Eno’s Music for Airports, a part might be a single sustained note, a short phrase, or a slowly changing texture. As parts return at different intervals, they meet in new combinations.

Leave room for those encounters. Your recording doesn’t need to fill every moment or sound complete on its own. Space, silence and a few carefully chosen notes can contribute just as much as a melody.

  • Moonlight: warmth, space and expression for verses and choruses.
  • Dawn: energy, movement and fullness for bigger choruses and bridges.

Any instrument or sound is welcome. Your part can suit either stage—or both.

Hear the idea in action

Explore Reverb Machine’s breakdown of Brian Eno’s Music for Airports. Try the individual loops and timing controls to hear how simple parts create changing combinations.

1. Add your recordings

Bring your ideas here to try. Add each part as a separate audio file so you can hear it on its own and alongside others.

Record in G, keeping any silence and reverb tails you want to hear. Your audio will play at its original pitch and speed.

You can name and tag your recordings after adding them.

MP3, WAV and other formats your browser supports. Up to 20 files, 30 MB each and 10 minutes per phrase. Files stay in this tab. Save a session before leaving to keep your audio and settings. Nothing is uploaded or submitted.

2. Listen and arrange

Choose an atmosphere to hear your recordings in context. Preview one recording, select several to play together, or explore random combinations.

Tag recordings Moonlight, Dawn, or both to include them in that stage’s random playback.

Turn the backing cello on or off, or listen to your recordings alone. Take your time—leave it playing and notice which combinations work.
